| OPEN-17 | Collision risk assessment, 8 Nov, against HLK-LD2450 MIO checklist section 3 (ESSENTIAL: "MIO selection considers more than just the nearest distance", "both lateral (X) and forward (Y) position influence risk", "distinguishes approaching, receding, and crossing targets") and the reachable part of section 6 (ADVANCED: "previous positions and timestamps are stored", "targets are associated across consecutive frames", "relative X/Y velocity is estimated", "time to closest approach... is estimated", "predicted clearance is compared against a configurable safety radius", "handles stationary and near-zero-relative-speed cases"). Previously MIO selection used only the sensor's own radial distance and radial closing speed (OPEN-16); a target walking across the corridor (lateral motion, roughly constant radial distance) was invisible to it. Added: each frame, every target's `(x_mm, y_mm)` is matched to the closest *previous*-frame position within `THREAT_ASSESSMENT_ASSOC_GATE_MM` (400 mm) — checklist section 5 is explicit that LD2450 slots are not stable identities frame to frame, so this matches by position, not by slot index (`threat_assessment_associate`). From a match, a 2D velocity gives time-to-closest-approach and a predicted closest-approach position (`threat_assessment_predicts_collision`); when that predicted point falls inside `THREAT_ASSESSMENT_SAFETY_RADIUS_MM` (400 mm) within `THREAT_ASSESSMENT_TCA_HORIZON_MS` (3 s), the target's *selection* distance is reduced by a flat `THREAT_ASSESSMENT_COLLISION_BONUS_MM` (600 mm) bonus (`threat_assessment_effective_risk_mm`) — enough, by construction, to let a farther approaching/crossing target outrank a nearer stationary one, which is the literal test the checklist's section 9 asks for. The real, unadjusted distance is still what gets reported to Audio Alert/BLE and fed into the hysteresis/TTC history above (OPEN-14's constants, OPEN-16's STOP cue) — the risk-adjusted value is a selection heuristic only, never output. `threat_assessment_candidate_wins` (OPEN-16) now compares this risk-adjusted distance instead of raw distance for both the "outside the band" and "near-tie" cases, which generalises OPEN-16's tie-break rather than replacing it: when neither target is on a predicted collision course, risk distance equals real distance and the result is identical to OPEN-16's original behaviour (confirmed by OPEN-16's own tests, unchanged and still passing). All integer math (no floats, embedded-friendly): a target's velocity, time-to-closest-approach, and predicted position are mm / mm·s⁻¹ / ms throughout, using `int64_t` intermediates only where the products could overflow 32 bits. New unit tests in test_threat_assessment.c: a farther crossing/approaching target outranking a nearer safe one (the checklist's named case), a receding target confirmed NOT to get the bonus despite being physically close, and a first-frame case confirmed to fall back to plain nearest (no crash or bogus velocity from having no previous match). DRAFT and explicitly out of scope: `THREAT_ASSESSMENT_ASSOC_GATE_MM`, `_SAFETY_RADIUS_MM`, `_TCA_HORIZON_MS` and `_COLLISION_BONUS_MM` are all reasoned bench-guesses, not measured against a real crossing walk (staging a second person/object to walk across the corridor on cue is impractical solo); association is single-nearest-neighbour per frame, not persistent object IDs — it can mis-match two targets that cross paths closely, a known, documented limitation, not a bug; and head motion / the user's own movement are NOT accounted for anywhere in this calculation (checklist section 6's last item) — the sensor is assumed fixed relative to the user's forward direction for the whole prediction, which is false whenever the user turns their head mid-approach.  | OPEN-18 | Persistent track IDs, 8 Nov, against HLK-LD2450 MIO checklist section 6 (ADVANCED: "targets are associated across consecutive frames"), replacing OPEN-17's one-frame-back lookup. Previously each target searched independently for its single nearest match in the immediately previous frame (threat_assessment_associate, now removed) - two problems: (1) two targets could both claim the same previous position if they happened to be near each other, with no mutual exclusion, and (2) a target that read invalid for even one frame (sensor noise, a momentary drop below a filter) lost all its motion history and was treated as brand new. Replaced with a real track pool: each frame, threat_assessment_assign_tracks() does a *greedy global* assignment between this frame's targets and up to RTE_RADAR_NUM_TARGETS persistent tracks - repeatedly taking the single closest (target, track) pair left within THREAT_ASSESSMENT_ASSOC_GATE_MM, removing both from the pool, and repeating - so no track can be double-claimed in one frame. A track survives up to THREAT_ASSESSMENT_TRACK_LOST_GRACE_FRAMES (3, about 300 ms) of consecutive misses before being dropped, so a briefly-lost target keeps its velocity history instead of restarting; the velocity/TCA math in threat_assessment_predicts_collision (OPEN-17) is unchanged except that it now receives its "previous position" from the matched track (which may be more than one frame old, after a gap) rather than always exactly one frame back - the existing dt_ms-based formula already generalises correctly to any gap size, so no special-casing was needed there. Deliberately NOT full (Hungarian/optimal) assignment: at RTE_RADAR_NUM_TARGETS = 3 objects, greedy and optimal assignment agree closely enough in practice (there are at most 3! = 6 possible assignments total) that the O(n^3) Hungarian algorithm's extra ~100 lines of non-trivial augmenting-path bookkeeping were judged not worth the risk/verification cost for the gain; greedy only mis-assigns when two targets are already too close together to tell apart by position, a case where even optimal assignment has no better answer because the input itself is ambiguous. New unit tests in test_threat_assessment.c: a track surviving one missed frame and still correctly predicting a collision using the pre-gap position (not a reset/zero history), and a track confirmed dropped (and NOT given a collision bonus) once its gap exceeds the grace period. DRAFT: THREAT_ASSESSMENT_TRACK_LOST_GRACE_FRAMES (3) is a reasoned guess balancing "survive a noisy frame" against "do not let a dropped track's history silently attach to an unrelated new object that reappears nearby" - not bench-measured. Known remaining limitation, unchanged from OPEN-17: this is still single-nearest-neighbour-per-frame greedy matching, not a full multi-hypothesis tracker - two tracks that cross paths closely enough to be mutually ambiguous can still swap identities; head motion is still not accounted for anywhere in this subsystem (see OPEN-17). | Putra |
